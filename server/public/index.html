The problem

The doorstep is where trust breaks.

Every day, parcels are marked delivered that never reached the customer, and parcels that did arrive are reported missing. When it happens, nobody can show what really happened at the door. It comes down to one person's word against another's.

When a delivery is faked

A rider photographs the parcel at the door, marks it delivered, and leaves with it. The customer never receives it, and the delivery record says they did.

When a delivery is denied

A customer receives the parcel, then reports it as never arrived to get a refund or a replacement. The rider who did the job right gets the blame.

Why a photo is not proof

Today's proof of delivery is a photo the rider chooses when and where to take. It shows a parcel near a door. It does not show whether the parcel was handed over, who took it, or whether it was still there a minute later.

  • Refunds paid on guesswork
  • Honest riders penalised
  • Customers who stop trusting the service
  • Hours spent on arguments with no evidence

Not a photo of a parcel.
A record of the handover.

How it works

From the road to the door, recorded.

The rider wears a small camera on their helmet and carries their phone, as they already do. Everything else happens by itself.

  1. 01

    The rider sets off

    The rider starts the delivery on their phone. From that moment the customer can follow them live on a map.

  2. 02

    Recording starts by itself

    200 m before the address, the helmet camera starts recording. No button to press, nothing to remember, nothing to skip.

  3. 03

    The handover is captured

    The camera sees what the rider sees: the door, the parcel, the person receiving it. Delivered only unlocks while it is recording.

  4. 04

    Sealed under the delivery ID

    The video is kept with the delivery it belongs to. It is opened only if someone reports a problem, and deleted after 30 days if nobody does.

Why it can be trusted

Built so it cannot quietly be skipped.

Starts automatically

Within 200 m of the address, on every delivery. The rider does not decide when.

Delivered stays locked

A delivery cannot be marked done unless the helmet is recording it at that moment.

Works without signal

In a basement or a lift, the helmet keeps the video and sends it as soon as it finds signal again.

Tampering is flagged

A covered lens, a disconnected camera, a missing video, or a delivery marked far from the address all raise an alert.

Tied to its helmet

Every viewing logged

Privacy

Recorded when it matters. Sealed the rest of the time.

A camera at people's doors has to earn its place. These are the rules it follows, for customers, riders and anyone passing by.

  • Only the last 200 m and the handover are recorded, never a rider's whole day.
  • A light on the helmet shows whenever it is recording.
  • Videos are sealed. Staff can open one only after a problem is raised on that delivery.
  • Every viewing is logged with the name of the person who opened it.
  • Videos are deleted after 30 days unless a problem was raised about that delivery.
  • A customer's location is shared only when they choose, only while their tracking page is open, and only for that delivery.
  • The tracking page shows the customer's first name only.
Questions

Good questions, plain answers.

Is the rider being filmed all day?

No. The camera records only from 200 m before each address until shortly after the delivery is marked done. The rest of the ride is never recorded.

Who can watch a delivery video?

By default, no one. Staff can open a video only after the customer or rider reports a problem with that delivery. The company's administrators can review videos for checks, and every viewing, by anyone, is logged with their name.

How long are videos kept?

30 days. After that they are deleted automatically, unless a problem was raised about that delivery.

What if there is no mobile signal at the door?

The recording still happens. The helmet keeps it and sends it as soon as it has signal again, oldest first.

Can a rider switch the camera off?

They can, but they cannot then mark the delivery as done: Delivered only unlocks while the helmet is recording that delivery. A covered lens, a disconnected camera or a missing video is also flagged for the company to check.

Will I appear in the video when I receive a parcel?

Yes. The handover, including the person receiving the parcel, is exactly what the record is for. The video stays sealed, is only opened if there is a problem with that delivery, and is deleted after 30 days otherwise.

Do customers need to install an app?

No. Customers get a tracking link that opens in any phone browser, or can sign in here to see all their deliveries and report a problem.

Is my location shared with the rider?

Only if you press Share my location on your tracking page. It is shared only while that page is open, and only with the rider and the company for that one delivery.

What happens when I report a problem?

The delivery company checks the video of your handover and replies on your delivery page. Because the video shows what happened at the door, there is no need to argue over whose word to believe.
